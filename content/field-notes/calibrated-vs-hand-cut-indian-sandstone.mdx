## Calibrated vs Hand-Cut Indian Sandstone: What Trade Buyers Should Specify

When a buyer writes a purchase order for Indian sandstone, one line matters more than most: calibrated or hand cut. Get it wrong and the stone still looks right in a photograph, but the laying crew on site will feel the difference immediately. This note sets out what each term means, where it comes from in the processing line, and which jobs call for which.

### What calibrated means

Calibrated stone has been passed through a machine so that every piece sits within a fixed thickness tolerance, typically plus or minus 2 mm. At KHADANE™ this is standard across paving at 18, 20, 22, 25 and 30 mm, and on gangsaw slabs at 18 to 30 mm. Dholpur Beige, one of our allied varieties, is regularly supplied calibrated at 20 mm in sizes such as 30x60 cm.

The benefit is laying speed. A contractor bedding calibrated paving on a mortar screed can work to a consistent joint height across the whole area, because the stone itself is not adding variation. This matters most on commercial patios, large driveways and anywhere a thin-bed or semi-dry mortar system is specified, where the tolerance of the stone needs to match the tolerance of the laying method.

### What hand cut means

Hand cut is the traditional edge and thickness finish, where the stone follows its natural bed and is dressed by hand rather than machined square. On a riven paving slab this gives a true top arris with a slight taper back in section, so no two pieces are perfectly identical in thickness. It is the regular edge on our natural riven and tumbled paving ranges.

This is not a lesser finish. It is the right finish for projects where the stone is meant to read as quarried, not manufactured, such as cottage gardens, heritage-style courtyards and informal paths. Hand-cut stone is usually bedded on a more forgiving mortar bed that can absorb the natural variation in thickness, which is standard practice for riven sandstone in both the UK and continental Europe.

### Edge and surface must match

One rule we hold to on every order: split faces take split edges, and sawn faces take dressed or profiled edges. A natural riven surface with a machine-cut edge looks inconsistent once laid, and a sawn, honed surface with a rough hand-cut edge undermines the clean line the finish is meant to deliver. If a buyer is specifying a sawn and honed patio, the edge should be machine cut or bullnose, not plain hand cut. Copings, steps and pier caps follow this same logic: bullnose or machine cut, with rockface or straight handcut as options on copings, but never plain hand cut.

### Where each one is used

- **Calibrated**: thin-bed laying systems, large format paving (900x600, 600x900, 600x1200), gangsaw slabs, Dholpur Beige at 20 mm, any project with a fixed screed depth
- **Hand cut**: natural riven paving, flagstone and crazy paving, stepping stones, roofing (always riven and hand cut), rustic patios and garden paths
- **Straight handcut and rockface**: heavier formats such as copings, kerbs and walling from 30 mm upward, where a tooled, squared face is wanted without a machined finish

### Thickness tolerance and what it means on site

Calibration is about thickness consistency, not colour or surface pattern. A calibrated batch of Kandla Grey will still show the natural variation in tone and riven figure that comes with any quarried sandstone. What changes is that every slab in the crate sits within 2 mm of the specified thickness, so the laying crew is not hand-packing mortar to compensate for a thick or thin piece.

For buyers quoting jobs with a fixed labour rate, this is often the deciding factor. Calibrated stone reduces laying time and the risk of lippage between adjacent slabs, which matters on public realm and commercial contracts where snagging costs come out of margin.

### A quarry-side view

Because KHADANE™ quarries, cuts and processes in our own units rather than buying in finished stone, we see both sides of this decision from the block onward. A block destined for calibrated paving is sawn to blank thickness first and finished on calibrating machines. A block destined for hand-cut riven paving is split along its natural bed from the start, so the texture that buyers want is already built into the piece before it ever reaches a cutting table.

This also affects how we quote. Calibrated orders are specified by exact thickness and tolerance; hand-cut riven orders are specified by a working thickness range, since the natural split will vary piece to piece within that range. Both are standard production, not special order, across our main export varieties including Kandla Grey, Autumn Brown, Slate Grey, Raveena and Raj Blend.

### What to put on your purchase order

To avoid back-and-forth at quote stage, specify:

- Variety (for example Kandla Grey or Autumn Brown)
- Format and size (paving, setts, slabs, and the dimensions)
- Thickness, and whether it must be calibrated or is acceptable as a hand-cut range
- Surface finish (natural riven, tumbled, sawn, honed and so on)
- Edge (hand cut, straight handcut, rockface, machine cut, bullnose)
- Destination port and quantity

With those details we can confirm within one business day whether the specification is standard production or needs a custom run.
